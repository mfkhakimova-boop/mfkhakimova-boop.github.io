My own product

Where2Go: a bot that solves a very real problem

Photos generated with Higgsfield Soul 2.0

“So, where are we actually going?” — the question that comes up pretty much every time my friends and I try to meet up. So many places, yet somehow nowhere to go. That’s the problem that inspired this bot. The idea is simple: everyone in the chat fills out a quick poll about their plans for the evening — food, drinks, budget, and mood. The bot ranks places from a shared database and serves up the top picks to match the whole group’s taste.

The first version ran on a simple weighted algorithm, which meant tagging every venue by hand. Well, AI is pretty great at taking that off your plate. Just type /discover cool raves in Berlin, and voilà — a list of places that fit the brief. Even better, AI tags each suggestion in the right format, so you can add it straight to the database and won’t have to spend tokens searching for it again.

Either way, people always get the final say — it’s a vibe or it’s not.

How it works

  1. 01Ask

    Someone types /vote in the group chat.

  2. 02Vote

    Everyone taps cuisine, drinks, budget and vibe, wherever they are.

  3. 03Pick

    The bot ranks places from the shared base and sends the top three.

  4. AIScout

    /discover finds new places in any city and tags them for the base.

Beyond the bot

What I love most about Where2Go is how quickly an idea can turn into something real. Just one hour after identifying a problem, I had a working prototype.

But what excites me even more is its potential. Today, it’s a bot that helps friends decide where to go. Tomorrow, it could become a travel companion, a local city guide, or a discovery platform connecting people with recommendations from local communities and independent reviewers.

And why stop at places to eat? There’s a whole world out there to discover.

Since then, making things by hand has become my way to unwind and express myself. I love surrounding myself with beautiful things and bringing my own sense of beauty into the world around me.

For me, creating something isn’t just about the final result. It’s about making something that feels right, looks right, and is uniquely mine.
